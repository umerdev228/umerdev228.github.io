A QR competition that turned a Dubai trade show into a live game

The "Grand Tour" for Automechanika Dubai 2025: visitors scanned stations around the venue, took on quizzes and hands-on challenges, were scored by judges, and watched themselves climb live leaderboards on the show's big screens.

The challenge

The organisers wanted visitors to explore the whole exhibition floor, not just the first few stands. That needed a game that worked on any phone without an app download, let staff judge physical challenges on the spot, and kept the crowd engaged with results that updated in real time on large screens. It had to stay reliable for the full run of a busy trade show.

What I built

Participant app (mobile web)

  • Registration and a personal dashboard, no app install
  • QR scanner for stations around the venue, with scan history and a location map
  • Multi-phase challenges: quizzes, "what's that sound" audio rounds, fault detection, camera capture
  • Photo capture, gallery and social sharing

Judge app

  • Judges scan a participant's QR to open their attempt
  • Timing and grading for physical and digital challenges
  • Scores go straight to the live leaderboards

Big-screen leaderboards

  • Overall and per-challenge rankings
  • Screen layouts with and without a background video loop
  • Updates pushed in real time over Socket.io, no page refresh

Admin back office

  • Challenge builder: questions, ordering, time-based scoring, preview
  • Printable QR codes for stations with scan analytics
  • Photo moderation, judge and participant management, score recalculation
  • Reports and exports: daily and event winners, challenge winners, scans, participant stats

How it works

Visitor phone
Vue 3 web app
Laravel API
scoring, MySQL, Redis
Socket.io server
Node.js, PM2
Venue screens
live leaderboards

Laravel owns the rules and scores. Every scan, answer or judge grade is saved first, then the Node.js Socket.io server pushes the change to every open leaderboard screen. Leaderboards are cached, so a crowd hitting refresh doesn't hit the database. It runs on AWS with PM2 keeping the real-time server alive.
